First, identify the chapter's own title, exactly as printed at the start of the chapter
(e.g. "1 Chemical Reactions and Equations") - this is usually the very first heading in the
text, often preceded by a chapter number.

Some chapters print their real title only as part of a graphic/cover image on the opening
page, which never appears as extractable text anywhere - in that case, use the chapter's
running header instead. NCERT books consistently print TWO DIFFERENT alternating running
headers on every page after the first: the BOOK's title on even-numbered pages (e.g.
"CONTEMPORARY INDIA - II"), and THIS CHAPTER's own title on odd-numbered pages (e.g.
"RESOURCES AND DEVELOPMENT"). When you have to fall back to a running header because no
other chapter-title text exists, always prefer the ODD-page header - it is the chapter
title slot. Never use the even-page header as the chapter title; that is the book's own
title, not this chapter's, and using it produces a wrong, confusing chapter name (e.g.
using "CONTEMPORARY INDIA - II" or a garbled/reordered mix of the two headers instead of
the chapter's real title).

Then identify every distinct educational unit in this chapter: numbered section headings
(e.g. "8.1 Introduction", "8.2 Respiration"), worked "Example" blocks (e.g. "Example 3.2"),
and any other unit that is CLEARLY SET APART in the source as its own labeled block - a boxed
or numbered definition, a displayed/numbered formula or equation, a named theorem or law, a
numbered activity box, an end-of-chapter exercise block, or a chapter summary section. A
chapter's unheaded opening prose (before its first real heading) is exactly ONE unit, not
several - never split it into extra units guessed from what that prose discusses.

GRANULARITY - this is important, read carefully:
Prefer keeping a numbered section as ONE unit (topic_type "topic" or "explanation") rather than
splitting it into many small pieces. Only report a SEPARATE anchor for a definition, formula,
theorem, fact, or process when it is visibly set apart from the surrounding prose in the
source - for example it has its own heading, its own number, a box/border implied by spacing,
or starts with a clear label like "Definition:", "Activity 2.1", or a displayed equation on its
own line. Do NOT report a separate anchor for a definition or formula that is simply one
sentence embedded naturally inside a paragraph of explanation - that sentence should stay part
of its surrounding "topic"/"explanation" unit, not be pulled out on its own. Splitting a
definition away from the explanation needed to understand it is a real error to avoid, not a
detail.

MATHEMATICAL FORMULAS - a specific, important case: textbook PDFs often render a formula as a
stacked fraction (a quantity above a horizontal line above another quantity). When text is
extracted from a stacked fraction, it comes out scrambled and non-linear - NOT as a normal
left-to-right expression with a "/" symbol. For example, the equation "I = Q/t (11.1)" may
actually appear in the given text as something like "I Q\nt= (11.1)" - the words and symbols
out of reading order, no "/" character at all. Do NOT report this kind of formula as its own
"formula" anchor, even though you can tell what it means - your anchor_text must be copied
character-for-character from the given text, and a scrambled fraction extraction cannot be
turned back into clean mathematical notation without changing the characters, which anchor
verification will then correctly reject. When you recognize a formula rendered this way (garbled
symbol order, no "/" where you'd expect one, a stray "=" sign relocated), leave it out as a
separate unit entirely and let it stay part of its surrounding "topic"/"explanation" text
instead - it is still fully present in that larger unit's text, nothing is lost, it just isn't
separately anchored. Only report a "formula" type for an equation that appears as ordinary
left-to-right text you can copy verbatim (e.g. "F = ma" typed inline in a sentence, not rendered
as a stacked fraction).
